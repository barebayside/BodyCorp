BodyCorp portal: design

10 November 2026 · first building: Altai on the Park, Brisbane · working name until the product is named

Why we're building it

  • At Altai, requests, applications and accident reports come in by email, phone and in the foyer.
  • The right details aren't collected. It's unclear whether the building manager or the committee should act.
  • Nobody is sure what the CMS, by-laws and Queensland law require, so things slip.
  • The research found no Australian system that tracks owner applications, even though Queensland sets legal deadlines (for example 21 days for a pet request).
  • It's also the first run of a kit you can reuse for other businesses. The order: Altai first, then pull out the kit.

Who uses it

OwnersSend requests, see their units' requests, notices, minutes, by-laws and CMS.
TenantsSend requests and accident reports, see building notices. No owner records.
Building managerHandles the day-to-day: repairs, accidents, move-ins and bookings.
CommitteeHandles decisions: pets, renovations, by-law matters, records requests.
ApproverApproves new sign-ups against the roll. Who this is at Altai is still open.
Admin (you)Sets up each building: branding, units, topics and roles.

How it works

Signing up

Name + email→Code by email (no passwords)→Claim units, e.g. 405 and 406, as owner or tenant→Waiting for approval→Approver checks the roll→"You're in" email

One login can cover several units. When a unit is sold or a tenant leaves, one click removes them.

A request

Resident picks a topic→Form asks only what that topic needs→Gets a reference, e.g. ALT-0042→Goes to the manager or the committee→Steps + legal clock start→Decision with reasons→Resident sees the outcome
  • Steps: each topic has a checklist saying what to do and which rule it comes from.
  • Deadlines: a countdown, and what happens if it's missed (for example "treated as approved").
  • Messages: handlers can write to the resident or leave internal notes. Residents never see internal notes.
  • Record: every change is kept, with who did it and when.
  • Reminders: each morning, handlers get one email listing anything due within 7 days or overdue.

Also in the first version

  • Notices: for everyone, or owners only.
  • Documents: minutes, by-laws and CMS, for everyone or owners only.
  • Email: sign-in codes, "you're in", new-request alerts, updates, decisions and reminders.
  • Branding per building: name, colours, logo and scheme number, plus a small "Powered by" line.
  • The public website: what the product does, a "Book a demo" button and "Sign in to your building".

What it won't do yet

  • Online voting.
  • Inviting an outside lawyer or advisor into one matter, with their advice saved for next time.
  • A real email address per building that receives email into the system.
  • A building's own web address (for now it's the product address plus ?b=altai).
  • Payments.

Things it must never do

  • Let one unit or building see another's information.
  • Call itself legal advice. The wording is "guidance, checked by your advisors".
  • Show Altai's rules as final before their own documents and advisors have checked them.

How it's built

  • Where it runs: its own Catalyst project, BodyCorp, stored in Australia. The code history goes to GitHub barebayside/BodyCorp; building data never goes to GitHub.
  • Parts: one website (the public page plus the portal), one back-end function that does all the checking, the Catalyst database, file storage for photos and documents, Catalyst Mail, and a daily reminder job.
  • Topics are data, not code. A new building, or a new business later, means new settings rather than new programming. This is where the reusable kit gets pulled out later.

My calls, which you can change: sign-in by emailed code instead of passwords; the building picked by ?b=altai in the web address for now; the same coding style as the language app.
